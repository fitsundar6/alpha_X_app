# Alpha X Progressive Overload Principles

## Core Principle
Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed upon the musculoskeletal and nervous system during training. Without systematic progressive overload, neuromuscular adaptation plateaus.

## Primary Overload Mechanisms
1. **Load Progression**: Increasing weight when target repetition ceiling is achieved with clean technical execution (RPE <= 8, RIR >= 2).
   - Upper Body Compound Lifts (Bench Press, Overhead Press, Rows): Increment by 1.25 kg – 2.5 kg.
   - Lower Body Compound Lifts (Back Squat, Deadlift, Leg Press): Increment by 2.5 kg – 5.0 kg.
2. **Repetition Progression**: Expanding reps within the target bracket (e.g., progressing from 8 reps to 10 or 12 reps at the same weight).
3. **Set Volume Progression**: Adding 1-2 working sets per muscle group across training blocks if recovery indicators are positive.
4. **Execution & Tempo Progression**: Increasing eccentric control (e.g., 3-second eccentrics) or pausing at the bottom position without altering external load.

## Deload & Consolidation Rules
- When an athlete logs RPE >= 9.5 on successive sessions or reports joint pain / high fatigue in Weekly Check-Ins, load must NOT be increased.
- Propose load consolidation (same weight with focus on execution) or a 10-15% reduction in volume.
